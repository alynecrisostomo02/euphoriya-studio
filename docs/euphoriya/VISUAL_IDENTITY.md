# Euphoriya visual identity

## Identity statement

Euphoriya is a living illustrated archive: a rare storybook, celestial manuscript,
fantasy atlas, character dossier, and interactive graphic novel sharing one visual
language.

Its interface may use modern rendering, responsive layouts, fast transitions, and
high-quality media, but it must not look like a generic modern SaaS product.

**Technologically modern. Aesthetically timeless.**

Primary qualities:

- vintage fantasy;
- mystical and enchanted;
- celestial and arcane;
- medieval-influenced without becoming generic medieval UI;
- illustrated and editorial;
- storybook-like;
- cinematic when the narrative calls for it;
- readable and calm enough for long creative sessions.

The default atmosphere selected for the main workspace is **mystical twilight**.
Regions, stories, characters, and periods may shift the atmosphere while remaining
inside the same Euphoriya language.

## Reference-board interpretation

The reference board supplied on 2026-10-03 includes map/quest interfaces,
character-focused fantasy interfaces, ornamental card systems, environmental
graphic-novel pages, and illustrated nocturnal city scenes.

The images are visual research only. Their origin and license are not established,
so they must not be copied into the product, traced, shipped, or treated as
reusable assets. Euphoriya adopts the principles below through original design.

### 1. Map and quest interface

Useful principles:

- the map is the primary spatial canvas rather than a small illustration beside a
  database list;
- filters remain close to the map and use compact symbolic language;
- selection opens a focused editorial detail card without abandoning spatial
  context;
- chapters, characters, map, and narrative objectives feel like views of one
  world;
- subdued terrain allows important locations and current context to glow.

Euphoriya translation:

- Euphoriya Atlas uses original maps, symbols, compass ornaments, region color,
  and project data;
- location details appear as manuscript/atlas annotations;
- filters cover regions, settlements, routes, people, events, secrets, and story
  periods;
- future POV/spoiler rules determine which annotations are visible.

Do not copy franchise iconography, quest terminology, map art, or controller-led
layout.

### 2. Character-centered cinematic shell

Useful principles:

- the character or environment may become the visual center while interface
  controls recede;
- a small number of decisive actions can hold more weight than dense toolbars;
- modal choices use strong image-led cards and clear selection states;
- background art establishes place before text explains it.

Euphoriya translation:

- Character Studio opens with portrait, name, species, epithet, and a compact
  identity constellation;
- character artwork is content, not decoration behind a form;
- deeper sections reveal progressively;
- the interface returns to quiet editorial surfaces during writing and detailed
  editing.

Avoid multiplayer lobby language, battle-first framing, giant generic PLAY
buttons, always-dark armor aesthetics, and game currencies.

### 3. Ornamental character and discovery interface

Useful principles:

- fine lines, stars, arcs, framed portals, and asymmetrical editorial composition
  can create an enchanted interface;
- a central character illustration can coexist with narrative text and actions;
- arched image cards feel like windows into events and places;
- glow works best as a precise selection cue, not a blanket effect.

Euphoriya translation:

- use original constellations, wings, seals, runes, botanical motifs, moons, and
  suns associated with Euphoriya;
- use arches selectively for memories, revelations, locations, and story portals;
- typography and spacing must remain readable in Portuguese and other future
  locales;
- decorative lettering belongs in titles, seals, and chapter moments—not long
  prose.

Do not adopt anime character proportions, gacha structure, currency bars,
recruitment loops, or copied Chinese title treatments.

### 4. Graphic-novel environmental storytelling

Useful principles:

- panel size controls rhythm;
- wide panels establish place and weather;
- narrow panels focus on gestures, clues, objects, and reactions;
- repeated viewpoints create movement without requiring continuous video;
- gutters, silence, light, and color can carry narrative meaning;
- environments can act as characters.

Euphoriya translation:

- Story Mode supports adaptable scene layouts rather than one infinite text column;
- a scene may combine establishing art, portrait reaction, dialogue, detail,
  reveal, and choice;
- panels reference structured scene, participant, POV, knowledge, state-change,
  and media data;
- the renderer can shift between book page, cinematic sequence, character
  portrait, atlas view, and interactive object;
- alternate choices remain separate from canon until explicitly promoted.

Do not lock the renderer to one comic style, fixed number of panels, black gutters,
or a single dark urban palette.

## Color system

The base system should support these families:

| Role | Direction |
| --- | --- |
| Paper/light surface | aged ivory, parchment, warm cream |
| Ink | softened near-black, deep brown, graphite |
| Primary twilight | moonlight blue, faded teal, deep blue-green |
| Nature | muted sage, moss |
| Character/accent | dusty rose, lavender, deep wine |
| Precious detail | antique gold, aged bronze |
| Revelation/light | pale celestial gold, cool silver, luminous ivory |

Pure black should be reserved for deliberate cinematic depth, graphic-novel
gutters, modal focus, and specific dark regions. It must not become the universal
application background.

Gold is an accent and hierarchy signal, not a substitute for every border,
headline, and selected state.

## Typography

Use three controlled roles:

1. **Display serif:** chapter covers, primary titles, character names, and major
   narrative moments.
2. **Readable editorial serif or humanist text face:** longer lore, scene,
   chronicle, and codex reading where appropriate.
3. **Clear interface face:** compact labels, controls, metadata, search, and
   accessibility-critical UI.

Ornamental lettering is limited to short titles, seals, runes, and decorative
labels. Never use it for paragraphs, forms, or small mobile text.

Typography must support Portuguese diacritics and remain legible at mobile sizes.
Roboto/Material defaults should not define the final Euphoriya identity.

## Surfaces and composition

Prefer:

- art-led hero regions with readable foreground layers;
- manuscript or atlas cards with restrained texture;
- thin ink rules and original ornament;
- asymmetric editorial grids;
- framed portraits;
- spatial hierarchy created by light, scale, and emptiness;
- contextual controls that recede when not needed;
- layered surfaces with enough contrast for long reading.

Avoid:

- admin dashboards dominated by charts;
- generic white cards on gray backgrounds;
- dense corporate tables as the primary archive view;
- every panel using the same rectangle;
- texture behind every text block;
- translucent layers that reduce contrast;
- large permanent sidebars on mobile;
- decoration that competes with editable content.

## Character Studio

The first integrated slice is identity, species, and portrait.

The intended opening composition:

- portrait as the visual anchor;
- name and species as the only required creation fields;
- optional epithet/concept nearby;
- visible autosave state without a dominant Save button;
- species chosen from the World catalog with quick creation available;
- progressive entry into Appearance, Psychology, Background, Relationships,
  Abilities, Knowledge, Beliefs, Secrets, Narrative, Timeline, Canon, Media, and
  History;
- inspiration shown only when requested;
- removal sends the character to a recoverable trash.

The archive view is a portrait gallery, not a corporate table.

## Story overview

Opening a project leads to story overview.

Primary emphasis:

- what happened so far;
- active arcs and conflicts;
- a connected narrative line for causes and consequences;
- knowledge viewed by chapter or scene;
- rumors visually distinct and carrying confidence;
- canon conflicts becoming revision proposals;
- character history combining visual chronicle and detailed timeline.

## Responsive behavior

### Desktop

- art-led workspace;
- contextual navigation and inspector panels;
- larger portrait/map/story canvas;
- keyboard-friendly creative flow.

### Mobile

- one dominant surface at a time;
- compact or bottom navigation;
- portrait and story media before secondary metadata;
- sheets/drawers for filters and inspectors;
- reading and quick editing prioritized;
- no desktop sidebar squeezed into the viewport.

Desktop and mobile are first-class targets. Tablet may use the desktop language
with collapsible two-column layouts.

## Motion and effects

Motion expresses material and magic:

- ink reveal;
- paper/page reveal;
- constellation line drawing;
- subtle card lift;
- controlled glow;
- dust, stars, leaves, or light motes;
- cinematic panel transitions.

Effects remain subtle by default and user-configurable. Follow the budgets and
fallbacks in [PERFORMANCE_PRINCIPLES.md](PERFORMANCE_PRINCIPLES.md).

Every experience must remain usable with reduced motion and effects disabled.

## Character-specific visual dossiers

A character may tint their own dossier without redefining the application-wide
theme. The shell, typography, spacing, accessibility, and interaction language
remain Euphoriya; palette, motif, portrait treatment, and ambient detail may adapt
to the character.

The supplied sheets are evolving visual concepts rather than frozen final designs.
They guide the current direction, while changeable costume, accessory, palette, and
presentation details must be confirmed in Character Studio before becoming final
structured appearance or canon data.

### Natasha

Visual reading:

- graphite, near-black, midnight blue, muted indigo, cold gray, and pale skin;
- elongated diamond motif;
- layered, flowing fabric and a controlled silhouette;
- melancholy, nobility, mystery, absence, and restraint;
- darkened hands and hooded presentation as potentially important character
  details;
- pointed ears are canonical; their absence in the supplied concept sheet is a
  visual error to correct in future art;
- Natasha does not possess wings; their absence is correct and must be preserved.

Dossier translation:

- quiet indigo atmosphere rather than pure black;
- elongated frames, slow ink reveals, and sparse cold highlights;
- portrait and expression studies prioritized over decorative effects;
- her visual darkness remains character-specific and must not set the global
  application palette.

### Emilly

Visual reading:

- ivory, warm cream, pearl, antique gold, and soft blush;
- sun/star insignia and iridescent accents;
- large white wings with cream and gold variation;
- noble, celestial, paladin-influenced structure;
- luminous and ordered silhouette.

Dossier translation:

- warm ivory surfaces, celestial gold rules, and very subtle prismatic light;
- wing studies, insignia, portrait, and costume construction as primary media;
- restrained light motes or constellation lines when effects are enabled;
- a brighter character atmosphere that still belongs to mystical twilight.

Pointed ears are confirmed as canonical to Natasha, Emilly, and Lilith. Emilly's
sheet depicts them correctly; their absence from Natasha's and Lilith's current
sheets is a concept-art error that future approved art must correct. Wings differ:
Emilly and Lilith possess wings, while Natasha does not. Lilith's current sheet
incorrectly omits her wings; Natasha's wingless silhouette is correct. These
character-level decisions do not by themselves establish species-wide anatomy
rules; those still require explicit lore decisions.

### Lilith

Visual reading:

- black, amethyst, plum, gunmetal, and cool silver;
- faceted crystal and angular diamond motifs;
- short dark hair, violet eyes, asymmetry, fitted combat clothing, and sharp
  accessories;
- confident, dangerous, precise, and contemporary-fantasy energy;
- pointed ears are canonical; their absence in the supplied concept sheet is a
  visual error to correct in future art;
- Lilith possesses wings; their absence in the supplied concept sheet is a visual
  error to correct in future art.

Dossier translation:

- amethyst selection glow, faceted separators, and sharper panel geometry;
- faster, more decisive transitions than Natasha's dossier while remaining
  subtle;
- costume/accessory details organized as media studies, not reduced to form rows;
- avoid letting the fitted black combat aesthetic turn the product into a
  universal dark-action interface.

### Future visual-profile boundary

When the existing data-core versions are reconciled, evaluate a small
character-visual profile or equivalent existing abstraction for:

- palette tokens or approved swatches;
- primary symbol/motif;
- portrait and gallery focal point;
- atmosphere preset;
- optional motion/effect cue;
- approved media references;
- costume/appearance study grouping.

Do not add a new table or migration solely from this document. Search for an
existing Character Studio/media/custom-field abstraction first and keep visual
configuration separate from canonical biography.

## Originality and reuse rule

Reference projects and supplied images provide composition and interaction
research, not a final skin.

For every visual decision ask:

> Does this make Euphoriya feel like a generic database, or like a universe I can
> create, understand, and explore?

Choose the second while preserving clarity, accessibility, performance, and
original authorship.
